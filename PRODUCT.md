# Product note

Students who encounter a broken question near an exam lose the most: they can spend scarce revision time learning an error and lose confidence in valid work. The product protects them by prioritising repeat reports, showing faculty exactly what would change, and responding clearly rather than silently editing content.

I would never let AI auto-apply a diagram, authored distractor, syllabus decision, chapter move, or an unsound-question rewrite. These choices need curriculum context and can harm many students if they are subtly wrong. Even permitted automatic edits are blocked unless a field-level replacement exists, the taxonomy permits it, and confidence is at least 0.92.

In week one I would watch **confirmed-defect precision**: the share of AI-routed defects that faculty approves. It measures whether we reduce faculty work without creating unsafe noise.

My next pitch: a “blast radius” monitor that estimates how many students saw a question since the first report and automatically escalates high-exposure, repeat-reported items to the top of the faculty queue.
